```card
---
Q: Which natural-deduction rule does the Lean tactic `intro` implement,
   and what does it do to the goal `P → Q`?
A: `→I`. It shifts `P` from the goal into the hypothesis context,
   leaving the goal `Q`.
```

```card

module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, propositions-as-types]
---
Q: Under propositions-as-types, a proof of `P ∧ Q` is what kind of
   value?
A: A pair `⟨hp, hq⟩` where `hp : P` and `hq : Q`. In Lean the
   constructors are `And.intro` / the anonymous pair syntax.
```

```card
id: srs-1.2-classical-only
module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, constructive]
---
Q: Name three propositional tautologies that require classical logic.
A: `P ∨ ¬P` (excluded middle), `¬¬P → P` (double-negation elimination),
   `¬(P ∧ Q) → ¬P ∨ ¬Q` (the hard direction of De Morgan for `∧`).
```

```card
id: srs-1.2-demorgan-or-fwd
module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, demorgan]
---
Q: State the constructively-provable half of De Morgan for `∨`.
A: `¬(P ∨ Q) → ¬P ∧ ¬Q`. A proof: `fun h => ⟨fun p => h (Or.inl p),
   fun q => h (Or.inr q)⟩`.
```

```card
id: srs-1.2-swap-asymmetry
module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, quantifiers]
---
Q: Which of `(∃ x, ∀ y, R x y) → (∀ y, ∃ x, R x y)` and its converse
   is provable, and why is the other direction unprovable?
A: The `∃∀ → ∀∃` direction is provable — the single `x` that works
   for all `y` also works per-`y`. The converse fails because `x`
   may depend on `y` in the premise; counterexample: `R x y := x = y`.
```

```card
id: srs-1.2-nonempty-distrib
module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, quantifiers]
---
Q: Why does `(∀ x, A x ∧ P) ↔ (∀ x, A x) ∧ P` require `[Nonempty α]`?
A: On an empty `α`, the left side is vacuously true, but there is
   no element of `α` to apply the universal to, so we cannot extract
   `P`. `Nonempty α` supplies a witness via `Classical.ofNonempty`.
```

```card
id: srs-1.2-cases-vs-obtain
module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, tactic]
---
Q: When do you use `cases h` vs. `obtain ⟨x, hx⟩ := h`?
A: `cases` for types with multiple constructors (like `∨`), producing
   one subgoal per constructor. `obtain` destructures single-
   constructor types carrying data (like `∃` or `∧`).
```

```card
id: srs-1.2-contrapositive-bhk
module: "1.2"
tags: [logic, bhk]
---
Q: Under BHK, what program does a proof of `(P → Q) → ¬Q → ¬P`
   correspond to?
A: A higher-order function: given `f : P → Q` and `g : Q → False`,
   return the function `fun p => g (f p)`. That's modus tollens as
   code.
```
