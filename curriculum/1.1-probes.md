# Probe Bank — Module 1.1 (Sets, functions, relations)

Used by `fmcourse socratic 1.1`. The LLM picks 5–8 probes, rephrases
as needed, and anchors follow-up questions to the "acceptable-answer"
hints.

## Probe: Set vs. its membership predicate
Difficulty: easy
Misconception it catches: treating sets as extensional containers
Seed question: "What does it mean, in Lean, that `Set α := α → Prop`?
State one consequence of this definition for how we prove `s = t`."
Acceptable answers must mention: sets are predicates; equality of sets
is propositional extensionality; we cannot count elements directly.

## Probe: Image vs. preimage
Difficulty: easy
Misconception it catches: swapping the two
Seed question: "A student says `image f s = {b | f b ∈ s}`. What did
they get wrong?"
Acceptable answers must mention: `image` quantifies existentially over
`a ∈ s` with `f a = b`; what they wrote is the definition of
`preimage`.

## Probe: Reflexive-transitive closure as LFP
Difficulty: medium
Misconception it catches: thinking the closure is syntactic rather
than the least fixed point of a monotone operator
Seed question: "In one sentence, characterize the reflexive-transitive
closure of `r` as a fixed point of some operator."
Acceptable answers must mention: the operator `F(R) = Δ ∪ r ∪ R∘R` (or
equivalent); least fixed point; Knaster–Tarski applies since this is
monotone on the powerset lattice.

## Probe: Why constructivism bit us in the De Morgan worked example
Difficulty: medium
Misconception it catches: thinking classical and constructive logic
interchangeable
Seed question: "The worked example `compl_inter_subset` used
`by_cases`. Why was that necessary, and what would fail if we worked
constructively?"
Acceptable answers must mention: `¬(P ∧ Q) → ¬P ∨ ¬Q` is not
constructively valid; we need `Classical.em` or `Decidable`; the witness
for which disjunct holds is not computable in general.

## Probe: Injective composition — generalization
Difficulty: hard
Misconception it catches: memorizing the proof instead of the shape
Seed question: "You proved `injective_comp`. State and prove the
corresponding result for bijections — without invoking composition
twice."
Acceptable answers must mention: define `Bijective := Injective ∧
Surjective`; compose via `And.intro`; injective-comp plus surjective-
comp handles each projection; no substantial new work.

## Probe: Why we prefer curried `Rel`
Difficulty: easy
Misconception it catches: assuming `Rel α := Set (α × α)` is
interchangeable with the curried form
Seed question: "Give two concrete advantages of `Rel α := α → α → Prop`
over `Rel α := Set (α × α)` when writing proofs."
Acceptable answers must mention: pattern matching on arguments;
composition is function composition; no explicit product
deconstruction.

## Probe: Closure idempotence
Difficulty: hard
Misconception it catches: proving the easy direction and declaring
victory
Seed question: "You proved `ReflTransClosure (ReflTransClosure r) ⊆
ReflTransClosure r`. Why did we not also need the reverse direction?"
Acceptable answers must mention: the reverse follows from
`rel_subset_reflTrans`; idempotence in full needs both; this is the
nontrivial direction.
